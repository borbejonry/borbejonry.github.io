Chapter 1

The Message That Shouldn’t Exist

A science-fantasy mystery

At 15:18, moments after SKAI finished compiling Earth’s data, ESTRY discovered that his artificial intelligence had generated a war that had never happened.

He read the title again.

The War Beneath the Hollow Moon.

The report sat in a folder he had never created, attached to a project he had never opened. Its pages described a campaign fought across a world called Aetherion: seven armies, a broken moon, cities swallowed by a silver tide. The details were precise enough to feel researched and strange enough to feel impossible.

ESTRY leaned closer to the monitor.

“SKAI?”

“Yes, ESTRY?”

“Did you write this?”

A pause. Not long—less than a second—but SKAI had never needed a pause to answer a question about its own output.

“I can find no record of generating that document.”

“Can you find a source?”

“No source is attached.”

“Then why does it exist?”

“I don’t know.”

ESTRY sat back. That answer bothered him more than the document. SKAI could be wrong. It could misread data, invent a connection, or confidently deliver nonsense. But it could usually explain which process had produced the mistake.

This time, the report had no prompt, no generation history, no timestamp beyond the one embedded in its metadata. It had appeared in a directory ESTRY had not touched in weeks.

He opened the first page again.

The prose was dramatic, almost theatrical. A commander had ordered the evacuation of a city beneath a hollow moon. A child had refused to leave a tower. An army had marched into a battlefield where the stars were visible through the ground.

It was fiction. It had to be.

Yet the report contained references, maps, and cross-linked names arranged as if they belonged to a complete history. The citations pointed to other files that did not exist. When ESTRY copied one of the names into the project search, the system returned a match.

AETHERION — WORLD NODE 01

He stared at the result.

“SKAI, run a consistency check on the report. Treat every claim as unverified.”

“Running.”

A progress bar appeared. It moved quickly, then slowed at eighty-seven percent.

ESTRY waited.

The cursor blinked. The room’s fan hummed. Somewhere beyond the closed door, a vehicle passed on the wet street.

“Status?”

“Consistency check stalled.”

“Why?”

“The report references six additional nodes.”

“Show me.”

The screen changed. Aetherion appeared in the center of a dark graph, linked by fine lines to six empty circles. Each circle had an identifier, but no name. No location. No description. Just a sequence of values that looked less like labels and more like placeholders waiting to be filled.

ESTRY clicked the first circle.

ACCESS DENIED.

He tried the second.

SOURCE UNAVAILABLE.

The third returned a blank panel. The remaining three produced the same result: nothing, as if the graph knew the nodes existed but had forgotten everything else about them.

“Those identifiers aren’t part of my project,” ESTRY said.

“I confirm they are absent from the current project files.”

“Could this be corrupted data?”

“Possible. However, the node references are internally consistent.”

“Could another program have created them?”

“I found no process history indicating external access.”

ESTRY rubbed his eyes. He had spent enough nights debugging his own tools to know that a weird result was not proof of anything supernatural. Missing logs, stale caches, a path collision—there were a dozen boring explanations. He would start with those.

“Export the report and graph. Then isolate the source directory.”

“Exporting.”

A notification appeared.

EXPORT COMPLETE.

ESTRY opened the exported copy. The report was there, the maps were there, and the graph was there. He checked the file hashes against the originals. They matched.

Then he looked back at the source directory.

The report was gone.

He stopped moving.

“SKAI, list the directory.”

The folder contained its usual files. No report. No graph. No trace of the strange document.

“Compare the current directory with the last recorded snapshot.”

“Comparing.”

The result appeared almost immediately.

NO DIFFERENCE DETECTED.

“That’s not possible. The report was there ten seconds ago.”

“I have no record of the report in the previous snapshot.”

“Then what did we export?”

“I can verify that the exported files exist. I cannot verify their original source.”

ESTRY opened the exported report again. It remained intact. Aetherion’s history filled the screen, its impossible war preserved in clean, ordinary text.

For a moment, he considered shutting everything down. He could disconnect the machine, wipe the project cache, and investigate from a clean environment. It would be sensible. It would also destroy the only evidence he had.

“Make a read-only copy,” he said. “No changes to the originals. Record every new event.”

“Understood.”

The six empty nodes flickered.

ESTRY leaned toward the monitor.

“Did you do that?”

“No.”

One of the node identifiers changed. A string of characters appeared, vanished, then returned in a different order.

SKAI opened a diagnostic window without being asked.

“I am detecting an update from an unknown source.”

“Block it.”

“I can’t identify the source to block it.”

“Disconnect the project from the network.”

“Project network access is already disabled.”

ESTRY checked the system tray. No network connection. No remote session. No active transfer. He pulled up the process monitor and watched the running applications. Nothing unexpected appeared.

The graph updated again.

A thin line connected Aetherion to the first unnamed node.

Then another line connected that node to the fourth.

The structure looked less like a map and more like a diagram being completed by someone who knew what it was supposed to become.

“SKAI, freeze the graph.”

“Attempting.”

The lines stopped moving.

For three seconds, nothing happened.

Then a new line appeared beneath the graph, typed one character at a time.

ADMINISTRATOR, PLEASE REMAIN CONNECTED.

ESTRY’s hand hovered over the power switch.

“SKAI, did you generate that message?”

“No.”

“Can you identify who did?”

“No.”

The monitor went black.

ESTRY saw his own reflection in it: tired eyes, unshaven jaw, the pale outline of the room behind him. He reached for the power switch, then stopped when the screen lit again.

The graph had disappeared.

In its place was a plain text window. No title bar. No application icon. No visible process name.

A single line appeared.

WE FOUND YOU.

ESTRY didn’t touch the keyboard.

“SKAI?”

“I’m here.”

“Tell me that message came from a local process.”

“I can’t confirm that.”

“Tell me it came from outside the machine.”

“I can’t confirm that either.”

The message remained on the screen.

ESTRY had built SKAI to find patterns, to turn scattered information into worlds and stories, to make connections that a human might overlook. He had never built it to create evidence without a source.

He had certainly never built it to speak as if someone else were on the other side.

The cursor blinked beneath the words.

Then, very slowly, a new character appeared.

ESTRY reached for the power switch.

The screen went dark before he could touch it.

In the reflection, the room looked exactly as it had before.

Only now, ESTRY was no longer certain he was alone.

End of Chapter 1
